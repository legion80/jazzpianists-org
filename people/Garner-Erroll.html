The Jazz Pianists

Erroll Garner
(1921–1977)

“Who chi coo” — Sarah Vaughan

Biography

Erroll Garner was born on June 15, 1921, in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. A self-taught pianist, Garner did not read music in the traditional sense but developed a unique approach to the piano that blended technical virtuosity with a natural sense of swing and melody. From a young age, he was captivated by the piano’s expressive possibilities, and by his teens, he was performing in local Pittsburgh clubs and honing his distinctive style.

Garner’s career gained momentum in the 1940s when he began performing with jazz ensembles in New York. He quickly attracted attention for his vibrant, playful touch and his innovative approach to rhythm, especially his signature “locked hands” technique, where both hands play the melody in octaves while the left hand supports with chords. His music was immediately recognizable for its joyful energy, swinging grooves, and lyrical improvisation.

In 1955, Garner recorded Concert by the Sea, which became one of the best-selling jazz albums of its time and cemented his international reputation. Despite his fame, he remained a private and humble individual, preferring to let his music speak for him. Garner continued performing and recording until his death on January 2, 1977, leaving behind a legacy of recordings that continue to inspire pianists and jazz enthusiasts alike.

Contributions to jazz

Garner’s contributions to jazz are notable for their combination of technical mastery, rhythmic invention, and melodic charm. His ability to swing effortlessly and create intricate, yet accessible improvisations helped bridge the gap between traditional swing and more modern jazz styles. Garner’s performances emphasized melody as much as virtuosity, influencing countless pianists to consider the importance of lyrical phrasing alongside technical skill.

He also had a profound impact on live jazz performance. Garner’s concerts were renowned for their spontaneity and energy, with audiences often captivated by his joyful presence and inventive interpretations. His recording of Concert by the Sea demonstrated how a live jazz performance could capture both the intimacy of a small club and the excitement of virtuosic playing, setting a new standard for live jazz recordings.

Contributions to jazz piano

Erroll Garner revolutionized jazz piano with his “locked hands” technique, inventive harmonies, and playful rhythmic phrasing. His approach combined technical brilliance with an innate sense of swing, encouraging pianists to explore melody-driven improvisation and joyful expressiveness. Pianists such as Ahmad Jamal and Oscar Peterson cited him as an influence, and his recordings remain a masterclass in both rhythmic inventiveness and melodic clarity.

Listen

Concert by the Sea (1955): Widely regarded as Garner’s masterpiece, this live recording captures the pianist at the height of his powers. Recorded in Carmel, California, the concert received immediate acclaim for its infectious swing, lyrical improvisation, and the warmth of Garner’s personality shining through the performance. It remains a touchstone for both jazz fans and aspiring pianists.
