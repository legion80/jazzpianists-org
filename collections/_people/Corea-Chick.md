{%- capture bio -%}
Chick Corea, born Armando Anthony Corea on June 12, 1941, in Chelsea, Massachusetts, was a prodigiously talented pianist and composer who became one of the central figures in modern jazz. He began piano lessons at the age of four and was heavily influenced by classical music, as well as by jazz giants like Bud Powell and Horace Silver. Corea studied at the Juilliard School of Music briefly before embarking on a career that would see him bridge multiple styles of jazz with a boundless creativity.

In the 1960s, Corea gained recognition as a sideman for prominent jazz figures such as Miles Davis, Stan Getz, and Blue Mitchell. His work with Miles Davis, particularly on the groundbreaking electric albums *In a Silent Way* and *Bitches Brew*, helped lay the foundation for jazz fusion. During this time, Corea also recorded extensively as a leader, experimenting with both acoustic and electric keyboards.

Throughout his career, Corea was known for his musical curiosity and versatility. He explored Latin jazz, avant-garde, straight-ahead jazz, and fusion, forming influential groups like Return to Forever, the Elektric Band, and the Akoustic Band. His career spanned more than six decades, earning him 23 Grammy Awards and countless accolades, leaving a lasting imprint on generations of musicians before his passing on February 9, 2021.

{%- capture contrib_jazz -%}
Chick Corea was a pioneer in jazz fusion, seamlessly blending jazz with rock, Latin, and funk elements. His work with Return to Forever in the 1970s helped define the fusion genre, introducing complex compositions, virtuosic solos, and electronic textures that expanded the sonic possibilities of jazz. He was also a prolific composer, writing pieces like "Spain", "500 Miles High", and "La Fiesta", which became jazz standards and are still widely performed today.

Corea also contributed to jazz by fostering collaboration and innovation. He frequently brought together musicians from different backgrounds, encouraging experimentation in group settings. Beyond fusion, his projects in acoustic jazz, such as the Chick Corea Akoustic Band, reaffirmed his command of traditional jazz forms and his ability to innovate within them. His openness to cross-genre exploration inspired countless musicians to push the boundaries of jazz.
{%- endcapture -%}

{%- capture contrib_piano -%}
As a pianist, Chick Corea was renowned for his technical brilliance, harmonic sophistication, and rhythmic inventiveness. He combined bebop-inspired lines with modal and Latin influences, often exploring polyrhythms and complex chordal textures. Whether on acoustic piano or electric keyboards, his playing was marked by clarity, agility, and a distinctive melodic sensibility. His approach expanded the vocabulary of jazz piano, influencing generations of pianists who sought to merge virtuosic technique with compositional depth.
