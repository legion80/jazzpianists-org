Ragtime emerged at the turn of the twentieth century as a piano-centered, fully notated music built on syncopation and formal clarity. Its characteristic left-hand patterns create a steady metric framework against which the right hand displaces accents and melodic emphasis. Though largely composed rather than improvised, ragtime introduced rhythmic tension as a primary expressive device, laying essential groundwork for jazz piano.
