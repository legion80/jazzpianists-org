The surge of blues influence in popular music during the 1950's also made its way into jazz. The blues-flavored bebop sound was sometimes called "funky hard bop," serving as a contrast, if not a reaction, to cool jazz.

Drummers returned as a propelling force in the ensemble. Pianists played melodies that were as hot as those in bebop, but their phrases, mimicking horns, were effectively slower and more legato in touch.

Popular "soul" musicians like Ray Charles also had big influence. Through his arrangements and performances, he successfully demonstrated the compatibility of combining gospel and blues with jazz.
